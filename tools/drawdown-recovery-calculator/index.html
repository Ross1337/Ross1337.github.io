Why losses hurt more than equal gains help

Drawdown recovery is the most underrated math in trading. A loss and the gain that undoes it look symmetric but are not, because the gain is earned on the smaller balance the loss left behind. Lose 10% and you need 11% to recover — barely noticeable. But the gap widens fast: lose 25% and you need 33%; lose 50% and you need a full 100%; lose 90% and you need a staggering 900% just to get back to where you started. This is why professional traders obsess over keeping drawdowns shallow. It is not caution for its own sake — it is arithmetic. The deeper the hole, the more disproportionate the climb, until at some point the recovery becomes practically impossible.

The formula
Recovery gain = drawdown ÷ (1 − drawdown). Small losses recover easily; deep ones explode.

Keep drawdowns shallow and recovery stays easy

The lesson is not to avoid losses — losses are unavoidable — but to keep them small. A trader who caps drawdowns at 10–20% only ever needs ordinary gains to recover and can keep compounding. A trader who lets a position run to a 50% or 70% loss needs a once-in-a-career run just to break even, and most never get there. Position sizing and stop-losses are not about being timid; they are about never digging a hole so deep that the math turns against you. Protect the downside, and the upside takes care of itself.
