Why expectancy beats win rate

Expectancy is the average amount you can expect to win or lose on every trade, once both how often you win and how much you win versus lose are taken into account. A trader who wins 90% of the time still goes broke if the rare losses dwarf the wins; a trader who wins 35% of the time thrives if the wins are large enough. Expectancy collapses both into a single number: positive means an edge, negative means the strategy bleeds money over time.

The formula
Expectancy = (Win % × Avg win) − (Loss % × Avg loss)

Win rate is a trap

The most common mistake is chasing a high win rate. What matters is win rate AND payoff together. The break-even win rate tells you the minimum win rate your reward/risk needs to survive: at a 2:1 payoff you only need to win 33% of the time. Aim for a positive expectancy and enough trades for it to play out — that is a real edge.

Frequently asked questions

Expectancy is the average profit or loss you can expect per trade over a large sample. It combines how often you win with how big your wins are versus your losses: Expectancy = (win % × average win) − (loss % × average loss). A positive expectancy means the strategy makes money over time; a negative one loses, no matter how good a single trade looks.
