How position sizing works

Position size is the number of units you trade so that, if your stop is hit, you lose only a fixed, pre-decided slice of your account. It is the single habit that separates traders who survive from those who blow up. The formula is simple: divide the money you are willing to risk by the per-unit distance from your entry to your stop.

The formula
Position size = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Entry − Stop)

The 1–2% rule

Most professionals risk no more than 1–2% of their capital on any single trade. At 1%, you would need 100 consecutive losers to be wiped out — which buys you the time for your edge to play out. Sizing, not entry, is what keeps you in the game.

Frequently asked questions

Position sizing is deciding how many units, shares or contracts to trade so the loss at your stop equals a fixed percentage of your account. It is the core of risk management — it controls how much you lose when you are wrong, which matters far more than how much you make when you are right.
