What Is Risk of Ruin in Trading?

Risk of ruin is the probability that a string of losses wipes out your account before your edge ever pays off. A profitable strategy can still ruin you — here is what drives the number, and how to push it toward zero.

Most traders measure whether they have an edge. Far fewer ask the question that actually decides whether they survive: even with a winning strategy, what are the odds that bad luck arrives in a row and ends the account before the edge can compound? That probability is your risk of ruin. It is the bridge between “this strategy makes money on average” and “I am still here to collect.” Borrowed from gambling and insurance, it is the single most under-appreciated number in trading — because a positive expectancy means nothing if variance bankrupts you first. This guide explains what risk of ruin is, the four inputs that move it, and how to drive it down to something you can live with.

What risk of ruin actually means

Risk of ruin is the probability that your account falls to a level you have defined as “ruined” — often a total wipeout, sometimes a drawdown deep enough that you would stop trading — at some point across a long sequence of trades. It is a survival statistic, not a per-trade one. Crucially, it is separate from whether your strategy is profitable. A strategy with a genuine positive edge can still carry a meaningful risk of ruin if you bet too large, because losses do not arrive evenly spaced — they cluster. Variance produces losing streaks far longer than intuition expects, and a deep enough streak early on can drain the account below the point of no return before the edge has a chance to work. Risk of ruin puts a number on exactly that danger.

The four things that drive your risk of ruin

Four inputs decide your risk of ruin. First, win rate — how often you win; higher win rates shorten the losing streaks that do the damage. Second, reward-to-risk — how big your winners are relative to your losers; bigger winners recover drawdowns faster. Together these two define your edge, and a negative edge means ruin is effectively certain given enough trades. Third, and most powerful of all, risk per trade — the share of your account you stake on each position. This is the lever that dominates the equation: doubling your risk per trade can take a near-zero probability of ruin and lift it into the double digits. Fourth, your ruin threshold — how far you let the account fall before you call it ruined. The uncomfortable truth is that the input you control most directly, risk per trade, matters more than the edge you work hardest to build.

The gambler’s ruin behind the math

Risk of ruin descends from a classic probability puzzle called gambler’s ruin: a gambler making repeated bets against a much larger casino will, given enough rounds, eventually go broke even on a fair game — and faster on an unfair one. Trading is the same problem with the signs flipped: you want to be the house, not the gambler. The way you become the house is by holding an edge (positive expectancy) and betting small relative to your bankroll, so no realistic streak can reach your ruin threshold. A worked intuition: risk 2% per trade with a solid edge and your risk of ruin can sit near zero; risk 25% per trade with the very same edge and a handful of consecutive losses ends you. Same strategy, same win rate, same reward-to-risk — only the bet size changed, and it changed everything. That is the whole lesson of gambler’s ruin: survival is a function of bet size, not just skill.

How to drive your risk of ruin toward zero

There are four levers, and they are not equally easy to pull. Improving win rate or reward-to-risk is hard and slow — it means becoming a better trader. Lowering risk per trade is immediate and entirely under your control, which is why it is the first place to act: cutting risk from 5% to 1% per trade collapses risk of ruin dramatically without you having to win a single trade more. The widely-used 0.5%–2% rule exists precisely because it keeps risk of ruin negligible for any strategy with a real edge. The fourth lever is structural: keep your edge genuinely positive (a losing strategy cannot be saved by sizing), and use fixed-percentage position sizing so every bet automatically shrinks during a drawdown, which bends the streaks in your favour. The practical workflow is simple — confirm you have a positive expectancy, then run your win rate, reward-to-risk and proposed risk per trade through a risk of ruin calculator and lower the risk until the probability is one you can sleep with.

Frequently asked questions

As close to zero as you can reasonably get — most professionals aim to keep their risk of ruin under 1%, and many treat anything above a few percent as unacceptable. Because ruin is permanent (a wiped-out account cannot recover), it is one of the few trading numbers where you want to be conservative rather than optimised. If a calculator shows your risk of ruin above a couple of percent, the usual fix is simply to lower your risk per trade until it drops back near zero.
